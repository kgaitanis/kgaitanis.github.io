Case Study · CREALOGIX

One mobile platform. Approximately 25 banking applications.

As Mobile Team Lead, I was responsible for a shared mobile banking platform and the apps delivered from it to a broad portfolio of Swiss financial institutions. I also led technical interviews as the team changed and grew.

One shared platform serving about 25 apps.

I evolved a shared mobile system used by institutions with different branding, capabilities, backend integrations and release schedules.

A change requested by one bank could affect the other applications or add another permanent special case to the shared codebase. Each release had to account for security requirements, older integrations, bank-specific configuration and institutions moving at different speeds.

Representative institutions

The application portfolio included many Swiss cantonal and private banks, including Zürcher Kantonalbank (ZKB), St.Galler Kantonalbank (SGKB), Aargauische Kantonalbank (AKB), Julius Baer and VZ.

My responsibility

Mobile technical leadership

Technical direction, architecture decisions and responsibility for the mobile team's engineering output.

Shared-platform evolution

Keeping common capabilities reusable without turning bank-specific requirements into permanent coupling.

Security constraints

Authentication, transaction signing and sensitive customer data placed strict constraints on the platform design.

Multi-client delivery

Coordinating an application portfolio where institutions evolved at different speeds and required different integrations.

Hiring

Leading technical interviews and helping rebuild a mobile team whose composition was changing rapidly.

Engineering judgment

Evaluating candidates for technical depth, problem solving and the ability to work effectively in a complex product organization.

Configuration-driven CI/CD

The complete build and distribution process for every banking app was automated and driven by configuration files specific to each institution.

Automated builds

Bank-specific configuration controlled how each application was assembled, reducing manual work across a portfolio of about 25 apps.

Modular composition

Optional functionality could be added or removed with a single configuration-line change. Face ID support was one example of a module selected this way.

Environment matrix

Each bank typically required five or six variants for testing, internal use, different backend environments and institution-specific configurations.

Signing and publication

Some banks entrusted us with their signing keys and allowed us to publish on their behalf. Others preferred to re-sign the delivered application and manage publication themselves.

Every delivery path also passed through a sophisticated application-hardening process. We used proprietary Promon tooling to obfuscate and protect the application code before distribution.

Team building

CREALOGIX was also where interviewing became a substantial part of my leadership role. I led interviews for incoming engineers and helped decide what skills the mobile team needed.

Across my career I have conducted roughly 100 engineering interviews. That experience has influenced how I mentor engineers, assess technical judgment and think about the qualities that make strong teams sustainable.

What changed in how I work

This role made me much more cautious about configuration and extension points. With about 25 applications depending on the same platform, a shortcut for one customer could become a maintenance problem for the entire team. I learned to judge architectural decisions partly by their operational cost across future releases.
